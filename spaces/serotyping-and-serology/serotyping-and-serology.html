Dual register
Plain telling · 6th–8th grade

Germs wear sugar coats. Each different coat has a name and number — that is a serotype. Coats that are alike sit together in a serogroup. A blood test can show whether your blood has learned a coat — that learning-test is serology. When a germ with that coat gets into the wrong place — the lungs, the blood, the coverings of the brain — it causes a named sickness, a disease. How sick you feel is the illness. Vaccines teach the blood a coat ahead of time, often by gluing the sugar to a harmless helper protein called a toxoid carrier.

Full detail · 13th grade

Serotype = capsular polysaccharide (CPS) type of Streptococcus pneumoniae, defined by the cps locus; the capsule is the serotype. Serology here runs in two directions: the PN23M panel is an antibody test (bead-based multiplex immunoassay for IgG against 23 capsules), while a cryptococcal antigen test is an antigen test — same fluids, opposite directions of looking [D]. Conjugate vaccines (CRM197-linked) convert a T-independent polysaccharide response into functional, opsonophagocytic IgG with memory; PPSV23 (plain polysaccharide) asks only for binding IgG, with no T-cell help and no booster effect [D]. Disease terms in the Disease Table are vaccine-level, not per-serotype — no retrieved label assigns a different Latin disease name per serotype.

Kept separate, on purpose

The six tracks

Douglas's standing rule: these six are separate tracks for thinking — technically one at the root, never collapsed into each other in the tables. Vaccine ≠ virus ≠ disease ≠ illness ≠ sickness ≠ infection.

TRACK 1

Virus

The viral agent itself and its family — e.g. La Crosse (bunyavirus, California serogroup), St. Louis (flavivirus), Eastern & Western Equine (alphaviruses). Cross-reactivity follows family, not panel order [D].

American first: La Crosse virus · Latin second: Orthobunyavirus
TRACK 2

Disease

The named condition in the record or on a vaccine label — invasive pneumococcal disease, pneumonia, meningitis, otitis media, La Crosse encephalitis. Disease terms here are vaccine-level [D].

American first: Lou Gehrig's disease pattern · Latin second where the record gives it
TRACK 3

Illness

How the disease shows in the person. Bacterial meningitis and viral encephalitis share a showing — fever, headache, altered mental status, meningismus [D] — which is why the meningitis track links serotypes to all four analytes.

The showing is shared even when the pathogen differs [D]
TRACK 4

Sickness

Filed as its own track beside disease and illness. Douglas's thesis (§208): disease and sickness are technically the same thing — kept as separate tracks for thinking, one at the root [H].

Separate track · same root [H]
TRACK 5

Infection

The sixth track, added Oct 18, 2026 (§209). Douglas's definition: group bacterial, fungal, and yeast under infection [H] — while bacteria / fungi / yeast / mold themselves stay separate inside it.

Infection = bacterial + fungal + yeast, grouped [H]

Vaccine

Plain telling

Think of the value as how loudly the blood answered for that coat. Douglas draws his own line low, at 0.1, to sort the answers into groups for study. The lab draws its line at 1.0. Only one coat — 10A — answered above the lab's line. That is counting, not a grade on anyone's health.

In all five vaccines: 3, 7F, 19A. PPSV23 only: 2, 20. PCV21 carries 20A, not 20 — cross-protection against serotype 20 is unestablished [GAP]. PCV20's serotypes are grown in soy peptone broth [D].

Streptococcus Species — Group A vs Group B · Nov 4, 2026

Group A is not Group B — and neither is the panel

First, the correction that file leads with: PN23M tests one species only — Streptococcus pneumoniae and its capsules. It does not test S. pyogenes, S. agalactiae, S. mitis, or any other streptococcus; the Mitis-group relatives are a bridge only through cross-reactivity. Group A and Group B are different species, separated by the Lancefield cell-wall carbohydrate — not variants of one species. Both are beta-haemolytic Gram-positive cocci in chains, so shape and haemolysis alone cannot tell them apart.

Vaccine and Toxoid Comparative Matrix · Nov 5, 2026

One trick, many germs: sugar coat + toxoid carrier

Douglas's table, tested row by row by Ember. Verdict: it holds at the 51% bar with one correction — the pneumococcal carrier cell. Toxoids in his fourfold framing (Nov 4, 2026): parts of the disease, parts of the vaccine, parts of the human immunity response, and part of the key [H].

Plain telling

Take the sugar coat off a germ, glue it to a harmless toxoid helper, and the immune system learns the coat and remembers it. Pneumococcus, meningococcus, Hib, typhoid, and Group B strep all use that trick. Meningococcus B's coat looks too much like a human nerve protein, so it uses proteins instead. For the fungus Cryptococcus, the same trick is still mouse research only — no licensed fungal vaccine yet.

Full detail

Conjugation converts a T-independent polysaccharide antigen into a T-dependent one: CRM197 is a non-toxic diphtheria-toxin mutant; TT and DT are formalin-inactivated toxins that serve both as vaccines in their own right (DTaP/Tdap) and as carrier platforms. A carrier is attached to the polysaccharide — it is not intrinsic to the serotype. MenB required recombinant proteins (fHbp, NadA, NHBA) and OMV because its capsule, poly-α-2,8-N-acetylneuraminic acid, mimics human NCAM [D].

The cousin rules [D] — the documented shape of his top/bottom pattern [H→D]. Flavivirus antibodies cross-react across the family (dengue, Zika, West Nile, St. Louis, Japanese encephalitis, yellow fever), and asymmetrically: St. Louis antibodies light up West Nile antigen more than the reverse — one positive implying another, in one direction, exactly the shape of Douglas's top/bottom pattern. The mechanism (shared envelope-protein epitopes) is documented; that his pattern is this mechanism is his thesis [H]. The two alphaviruses (EEE/WEE) cross-react with each other, not with flaviviruses; La Crosse cross-reacts within the California serogroup — where his Jamestown Canyon pairing sits, same family, not on his panel [H]. His panel rename hangs on the La Crosse analyte: "Encephalopathy Meningeal Testing" [H].
