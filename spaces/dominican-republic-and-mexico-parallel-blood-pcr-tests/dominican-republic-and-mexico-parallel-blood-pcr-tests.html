The DR & Mexico tests your US panels don’t cover

Your two known US panels leave out the main Dominican Republic and Mexico arboviruses. The parallels are not one international panel — they are separate blood PCRs, public-health send-outs, and in-country reference labs, each with its own code, specimen, and ordering route.

General laboratory information only, not a recommendation for any person. Research status: index only — not verified live in a research browser. Re-check any test, code, and approval requirement with the performing laboratory at order time. A clinician and the performing laboratory must confirm availability, specimen, and timing.

Mayo ARBOP does not cover them

ARBOP — Arbovirus Antibody Panel, IgG and IgM, Serum is serum IFA for only 4 viruses: California (La Crosse), Eastern equine, St. Louis, and Western equine.

It contains no dengue, Zika, chikungunya, West Nile, Oropouche, Mayaro, yellow fever, or Venezuelan equine test — and it is antibody, not PCR.

Mayo CSFME contains no arbovirus at all

CSFME — Meningitis/Encephalitis Pathogen Panel, PCR, Spinal Fluid is the BioFire FilmArray panel, CSF only, 14 targets.

For a DR/Mexico exposure, the meningitis/encephalitis gap is not a different bacterial panel. It is the arboviral CNS gap — see §1 below.

Scope you set — kept throughout this page: Blood PCR only (serum, plasma, or EDTA whole blood). CSF-only panels are listed only in the Excluded section, to show what was ruled out and why — they are not presented as options. Serum antibody tests are blood tests but are not PCR, so they are noted separately and are not in the recommended list. “Mail” means a clinician draws blood and the clinic ships it to a reference lab — no direct-to-patient home blood-PCR kit for these arboviruses was verified.

Why arboviruses are the CNS gap

Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, West Nile, St. Louis, Eastern / Western / Venezuelan equine, Oropouche and Mayaro can all present with CNS disease — chikungunya and Zika with meningoencephalitis, myelitis and Guillain-Barré; Oropouche rarely with meningitis/encephalitis; dengue with meningoencephalitis, transverse myelitis, ADEM and Guillain-Barré — and none is on CSFME.

2 · What circulates in each country

Dominican Republic

  • Dengue is endemic, with regular outbreaks. 2026 Ministry data cited in press (epidemiologic week 41, 11–17 Oct) reported 474 confirmed cases in 2026 vs 265 in the same period of 2025, still described as within the endemic corridor.
  • Zika and chikungunya are emerging — chikungunya in 2014 and Zika in 2016 were the first/only reported outbreaks of each at the time of a 2021 analysis.
  • Oropouche risk is flagged in general DR travel guidance, but no DR-specific Oropouche endemic case count was verified — do not overstate DR Oropouche endemicity.
  • Meningococcal disease occurs but fell from 18 cases in 2016 to 2 in 2021 (Global Meningococcal Initiative summary). No DR-specific meningitis panel was found.

Mexico

  • Dengue is hyperendemic — all four serotypes DENV-1–4 circulating, with DENV-3 predominance in 2023 (63.9% in one surveillance analysis).
  • Chikungunya first recorded 2014, epidemic 2015, then declining; Zika first reported in southern Mexico Nov 2015, peak 2016, then declining; both now considered endemic with possible outbreaks.
  • West Nile is present in several Mexican states but only 13 officially confirmed human cases 2002–2023.
  • Venezuelan equine encephalitis is described as endemic in the Americas and “hidden under the dengue umbrella” — an epidemiologic claim, not proof of a routine Mexican human test.
  • Serologic traces of Mayaro in Veracruz; historical yellow fever. T'Ho virus appeared in a Chiapas bat/opossum PRNT study — a research finding, not a routine human blood-PCR test.

Americas-wide co-circulation context: dengue, chikungunya, Zika, plus Mayaro, Oropouche and yellow fever are described as concurrently circulating in the Americas.

4 · Who actually offers what — and the PRNT bottleneck

Mayo Clinic Laboratories

Commercial send-out, provider-ordered: DENGS, CHIKS, VZIKS, WNVS, POWVS serum PCR. Mayo’s own mosquito-borne algorithm splits by exposure: US → ARBOP/ABOPC; outside-US → add CHIKV, CHIKS, CHIKC, DENGM, DENVP, DNSAG, DENGS, DENGC, VZIKM, VZIKU, VZIKS; WNS/WNC in any region.

No Mayo blood PCR found for: Oropouche, Mayaro, Venezuelan equine, Western equine, Eastern equine, St. Louis, yellow fever, Jamestown Canyon, California serogroup (other than antibody). No Mayo Oropouche or Mayaro code was found.

CDC / US public health

Travel Panel 6150, Summer Panel 5470, Florida Trioplex 1550 and individual codes, CDC Oropouche and Mayaro. Neutralisation (PRNT) is the key public-health-only method — Philadelphia guidance: “Neutralization tests that help differentiate arboviral pathogens are only available through public health laboratories.”

Not orderable by a patient directly; requires clinician + state health department + prior approval for many viruses, and acute timing (<7 days for most RT-PCR, <5 days for Florida EEE/SLE/WN serum, <14 days for Florida Trioplex serum).

Other commercial US labs

Quest Oropouche PCR (July 2025). NYC guidance lists ARUP, Eurofins-Viracor, Labcorp, Mayo, Quest as preferred commercial options for dengue/chik/Zika IgM/PCR/NS1. Exact Quest/Labcorp/ARUP codes for dengue/chik/Zika PCR were not extracted — a gap; do not invent them.

Local-only / in-country & Canada

Mexico: InDRE network Triplex RT-qPCR. DR: Dr. Defilló DENV RT-qPCR + sequencing. No verified US mail-order route, code, or patient-facing ordering page found for either.

Canada: Public Health Ontario VEE and EEE serology + PCR — Ontario-clinician route only, PHO microbiologist approval, referred to CDC Fort Collins / Canada’s National Microbiology Laboratory. Not a US patient mail-order service.

5 · Equine encephalitis viruses — human blood parallels

“Equine” means the virus was first associated with equine disease — not that the test is for horses. Human and veterinary tests share virus names but have different codes, labs, and validity.

⚠ Veterinary trap — do not order an animal test for a human

Zoologix S0054 / S0055 / S0056 (VEE / EEE / WEE RT-PCR, EDTA whole blood or CNS tissue), Guelph eeepn, xweevvn, and Texas A&M TVMDL 8186 / 8055 are animal tests on equine assay sheets. They are listed here only so they are not mistaken for human parallels.

Eastern equine (EEEV)

Non-PCR: Mayo EEEP antibody (ARBOP component).

Venezuelan equine (VEEV)

Public-health referral

PHO VEE Serology and PCR — PCR on serum, minimum 5.0 mL blood or 1.0 mL serum, red-top or SST; requires PHO microbiologist approval; performed at CDC Fort Collins if criteria are met; turnaround set at submission (not routine). CSF form excluded.

Timing limit (BMJ Best Practice): viraemia may be detected only up to 3 days after onset; ELISA cross-reacts with other alphaviruses and needs PRNT confirmation.

No Mayo, Florida, or Wadsworth human VEE PCR code was found — in North America it is effectively a public-health referral, not a commercial panel item.

Madariaga (MADV)

No distinct code found

Madariaga is the reclassification of former South American EEE lineages II–IV (see naming, §8). No distinct human blood-PCR code was found.

Whether an EEE assay detects Madariaga depends on primers — that is speculation, marked as such; do not assume Florida 1682 or PHO EEE PCR covers it. The only defensible route on evidence found is to ask CDC Fort Collins / a reference lab — unverified.

Human disease documented in Haiti (2015–16) and Panama (2010, 14 cases, mainly children) — a real, if rare, human pathogen in the region.

Also in this differential: St. Louis — Florida SLE 1686, acute serum <5 days; otherwise ARBOP antibody only. Jamestown Canyon / California serogroup — Wadsworth lists PCR as CSF only, requires NPL approval, so excluded from the blood list. 7 · Can a spinal-fluid PCR panel be run on blood?

Short answer from what laboratories actually do: a CSF-validated assay is not automatically valid on blood. Changing the specimen changes the test’s regulatory status and requires a new validation. No laboratory was found offering the BioFire or QIAstat meningitis/encephalitis panel validated on blood as a clinical test.

~90% / ~50%Sensitivity when a blood-culture panel (BCID2) was repurposed for CSF in a study — ~90% when Gram stain positive, only ~50% when Gram stain negative. Repurposing is not equivalence.
87% vs 26%West Nile PCR sensitivity: whole blood up to 87% vs serum 26% (Mayo’s own page) — even within blood fractions, the matrix changes the answer.
26% vs 9.2%Enterovirus RT-PCR positivity in CSF without red cells vs with red cells — blood contamination itself inhibits the test.

Physically possible ≠ clinically validated

PCR chemistry does not know what tube the sample came from — a lab could physically produce a signal. But the panel’s published performance (~90% sensitivity, 97% specificity in CSF) would not apply, and the result would need a disclaimer and director-approved validation to be reportable.

Under CLIA (42 CFR 493.1253), a modified FDA-cleared test needs full establishment of performance before reporting: accuracy, precision, analytical sensitivity, analytical specificity including interfering substances, reportable range, and reference interval — in the true matrix (real blood, not CSF). Changing specimen type is explicitly listed as a modification that moves a test into LDT-style validation.

What labs do instead

  • Validate each specimen separately: Mayo has CHIKS (serum) and CHIKC (CSF); DENGS and DENGC; POWVS / POWVC / POWVU — separate IDs, volumes, and limits of detection. If swapping were accepted, none of that would be necessary.
  • Validate multi-matrix from the start: CDC Trioplex was authorised for serum, whole blood, urine, CSF and amniotic fluid — prospective validation, not retrospective adaptation.
  • Study repurposing and report the loss (BCID2-on-CSF study, left) — presented as a study of utility, not a validated clinical offering.

Documented limits on blood

Matrix inhibitors: heme/haemoglobin binds DNA polymerase and inhibits amplification; immunoglobulins, lactoferrin, heparin, bilirubin and lipids are also documented inhibitors in blood/plasma/serum, while CSF rarely has endogenous inhibitors. Diluting to reduce inhibitors also dilutes the target below the limit of detection.

Different limits by specimen, same assay: HSV PCR limit of detection is higher in blood (30 TCID50/mL) than in CSF (5–40) in the Children’s Minnesota listing.

Compartment biology — the decisive limit: many meningitis/encephalitis pathogens cause CNS disease without the pathogen being in blood at testing time, so a negative blood result does not exclude CNS infection. The reverse also fails: HHV-6 integrates into host DNA and is detectable in blood in many healthy people, so a positive blood result does not prove CNS disease. Blood and CSF results are not interchangeable in either direction.

Not speculation: the CLIA requirement, the CSF-only intended uses, the repurposing sensitivity loss, the inhibitor mechanisms, and per-specimen LOD differences are all documented in the sources below. Speculation, flagged as such in the research: any claim that a specific lab could validate ME-on-blood successfully, what its sensitivity would be, or that a future multi-matrix cartridge will appear. Vendor marketing about “one cartridge handles CSF, blood, or swab” is not evidence of a validated blood ME panel.

8 · Is the US at the forefront? Why do names differ?

Forefront — a qualified yes

Yes for reference, multiplex, and FDA-authorised work: CDC Fort Collins is the US arbovirus reference lab; CDC’s Puerto Rico Dengue Branch developed Trioplex, “the first Zika PCR test to gain FDA authorization”; BioFire and QIAstat are the FDA-cleared CSF syndromic panels.

No for sole leadership: the US lagged commercially on Oropouche until Quest in July 2025, still routes Mayaro and Venezuelan equine essentially only through CDC, and has no human WEE blood PCR found — while InDRE is a WHO Collaborating Centre for Arboviruses, PAHO/WHO sets regional guidelines, and Fiocruz-type and European work (Institut Pasteur, altona kits, QIAstat EU 16-target version) is part of the same ecosystem. “Forefront” depends on the virus.

Different names usually do not mean different viruses. Four mechanisms explain most of it:

1 · Place of first isolation, kept

Zika (Zika Forest, Uganda), West Nile (West Nile district, Uganda), La Crosse (Wisconsin), Powassan (Ontario), Oropouche (Oropouche River, Trinidad), Mayaro (Trinidad), St. Louis, California encephalitis (first serogroup virus found in California, 1943). WHO’s 2015 best practices bar geographic names for new diseases — but do not rename diseases already in common usage, so these stay.

2 · Taxonomic renaming (2023)

ICTV renamed genus Flavivirus → Orthoflavivirus, with binomial species names: dengue → Orthoflavivirus denguei, Zika → O. zikaense, West Nile → O. nilense, yellow fever → O. flavi. Virus common names did not change. A lab still writing “Flavivirus genus” is using pre-2023 taxonomy, not describing a different virus.

3 · Serogroup vs species

“California encephalitis” is both a virus and a serogroup (La Crosse, Jamestown Canyon, snowshoe hare, Tahyna, Inkoo, Trivittatus, Guaroa…). ARBOP’s target is listed as “California Virus (La Crosse)” because La Crosse is the actual assay target, and cross-reactivity means a “California” result may reflect another member — hence reports as “California serogroup,” “California (La Crosse),” or “La Crosse” without implying three infections.

4 · Splits & old group names

EEE → EEEV + Madariaga: North American lineage I stays EEEV; Central/South American lineages II–IV are now Madariaga virus — so older Central/South American literature saying “EEEV” actually means MADV. Old “Arbovirus Group A / B” became Alphavirus / Flavivirus (now Orthoflavivirus) — and “arbovirus” itself is an ecological term, not a taxonomic group. Dengue is one species with four serotypes (DENV-1–4); Powassan lineages 1 and 2 (deer tick virus) are both reported as “Powassan” in one test.
