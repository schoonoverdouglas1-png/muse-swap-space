Pneumococcal Vaccines & Serotypes

Every pneumococcal vaccine taken apart into the serotypes it covers, the carrier protein each serotype is conjugated to, and the one thing that connects them all — the serogroup: the capsular polysaccharide family a serotype belongs to. The organism throughout is Streptococcus pneumoniae.

Read the evidence labels first

Nothing in this list is presented as more certain than the research behind it. Every entry carries one of these labels, and the labels come from checking each item against more than one source — never from a single website taken at face value.

DocumentedConfirmed by two or more independent primary, regulatory, or peer-reviewed sources that agree.
Partially documentedOne strong source, or sources that agree in part but conflict or are incomplete on the detail.
GapNo retrieved source settles the point. The point is not asserted — do not copy it into a book or paper as fact.

Provenance warning — the single-source serotype table

One serotype's grouping information in an earlier table came from a single website — described as the only place on earth where it was found — with no bibliography attached, drawn from the internet rather than entered by hand. Which serotype it was has never been named, so this page does not guess.

Because of that, every serogroup entry here is treated as externally drawn and bibliography-missing until a second source confirms it — and each one below shows whether that second source was found. A candidate matching that description was identified during the research: a commercial two-page PDF from Sunfire Biotechnologies, Pneumococcal Serotypes and Their Capsular Structures, whose Table 1 lists serogroups 1–47, whose Table 2 literally says “Info coming soon!”, and which contains no bibliography, methods, or discovery dates. It fits the profile — but it is not asserted to be the source. It is the prime candidate to check cell by cell.

That candidate table is also demonstrably out of date: written in 2022, it predates nine serotypes discovered since 2020, so it is missing 15D, 20C, 24C, 33E/G/H, 36A/B, 10D, and serogroup 48 entirely.

The vaccines, reverse-engineered

The seven named vaccines are not a simple ladder. Five of them form a strict chain — each one contains everything in the one before it, plus additions. PCV21 breaks the chain on purpose, and PPSV23 stands apart as the only unconjugated one. And “PCV10” is not one vaccine at all: GSK's Synflorix and Serum Institute's Pneumosil differ in four serotypes.

PCV21 breaks the chain

Capvaxive shares only 10 polysaccharides with PCV20 (plus its modified 15B). It drops nine PCV20 serotypes — 1, 4, 5, 6B, 9V, 14, 18C, 19F, 23F — and adds ten of its own: 9N, 15A, 16F, 17F, 20A, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B, plus 15C reached by cross-reactivity. It is an adult-targeted set, not a “PCV20 plus one.”

PPSV23 stands apart

Pneumovax 23 is unconjugated and is neither a superset nor a subset of any conjugate vaccine. Against PCV20 it adds 2, 9N, 17F and 20/20A — and 6A is the only PCV20 serotype it lacks.

Conjugate information — what each serotype is attached to

A conjugate vaccine works by chemically attaching each capsular polysaccharide to a carrier protein, so the immune system responds to it as it would to a protein. Almost every product here uses the same carrier, CRM197 — a non-toxic variant of diphtheria toxin from Corynebacterium diphtheriae. The one real exception is Synflorix, which uses three different carriers. Documented for every row.

Grouped by the one connecting thing: serogroup

A serogroup is an antigenic grouping in the Danish system (Statens Serum Institut): serotypes whose capsular polysaccharides cross-react with the same typing antisera in the Quellung reaction. The number names the group; the trailing letter distinguishes members — serogroup 19, for example, is 19F first, then 19A, 19B, 19C.

What a serogroup is not

  • Not a genetic cluster. Grouping by the cps gene locus disagrees with Quellung serogroups in places — in six cases, serotypes in one serogroup fall in different genetic subclusters, and nine subclusters mix serogroups. Serotype 14 is serologically alone, yet its cps locus is clearly related to serogroup 15's. Name the grouping system every time: “Danish/Quellung serogroup,” “cps-locus cluster,” or “PCR genogroup.”
  • Not vaccine coverage. Vaccinating against one member does not cover the group: 6B does not cover 6C/6D, 15B does not cover 15A/15F/15D, and 23F does not cover 23A/23B — except where a specific cross-reactivity claim is documented on the card.
  • Not a species or fungal group. The Mitis group of streptococci and the Cryptococcus fungal serotypes are separate systems — see Relationships below.

Single-serotype groups (in the serological sense, among vaccine-relevant groups): 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 14, 31. “Single” must always be qualified — a future cps variant could split any of them, as has already happened to groups 15, 20, 33 and 36.

Latin disease names — what is documented, and what is a gap

The organism's name is Latin: Streptococcus pneumoniae (Klein 1884) Chester 1901 — formerly Diplococcus pneumoniae (1920–1974). The carrier organisms are Latin too: Corynebacterium diphtheriae (CRM197), Haemophilus influenzae (protein D), Clostridium tetani (tetanus toxoid). Those binomials are the only per-organism Latin names this research can attach to the vaccines. Documented

Per-serotype Latin disease names: Gap

No source retrieved assigns a different Latin disease name to each serotype — nothing that gives serotype 3 one set of names, serotype 8 another, serotype 19A another. A targeted deeper search for Latin disease phrases (“pneumonia pneumococcica”, “meningitis pneumococcica”) returned no such nomenclature anywhere. Disease names in the labels are modern medical terms of Latin/Greek derivation, used in English: pneumonia, meningitis, sepsis, bacteraemia, otitis media, sinusitis, empyema, peritonitis, and so on.

So the honest table is one row per vaccine (below), not one row per serotype. A per-serotype Latin-disease table built from this evidence would be invented — do not publish one. Surveillance papers do associate serotypes with frequencies of invasive disease, meningitis, or case-fatality, but those are epidemiological associations that vary by age and country, not disease-name assignments.

Bacterial & fungal relationships — by name, not blurred

Five different systems get called “groupings” in this field. Keep their names separate: (1) Danish/Quellung serogroup, (2) cps-locus cluster / genogroup, (3) the Mitis group of streptococcal species, (4) vaccine group, (5) the fungal (Cryptococcus) serotype system. Only system 3 gives documented cross-species relationships for pneumococcal serotypes — and only for a few.

Bacterial: the Mitis group

S. pneumoniae sits in the Mitis group of viridans streptococci (about 20 species); its “Pneumoniae subclade” groups S. pneumoniae, S. pseudopneumoniae, S. mitis, S. oralis, S. infantis, S. peroris and S. timonensis. S. pneumoniae and S. mitis are thought to have evolved from a common precursor. Some of those commensal relatives carry capsule (cps) loci highly similar to specific pneumococcal serotypes — documented serotype by serotype, never generalise:

And a caution that runs through all of it: “carries a similar cps locus” ≠ “expresses a pneumococcal serotype.” Most S. mitis/oralis/infantis strains examined carry full-length cps operons, but only a minority react with pneumococcal typing sera.

Fungal: no mapping exists

Per-serotype fungal relationships: Gap

Cryptococcus neoformans and Cryptococcus gattii are encapsulated fungi whose capsules are primarily glucuronoxylomannan (GXM) — a different chemistry from pneumococcal capsule repeats. Their serotypes are A, D and AD (neoformans) and B and C (gattii), defined by GXM reactivity. No retrieved source maps any of those letters onto a pneumococcal serogroup, and none should be created — a table equating, say, pneumococcal serogroup 15 with a fungal serotype would be invented.

The single retrieved bridge is one chapter summary: the cryptococcal capsule “is known to share some antigenic determinants with certain pneumococcal polysaccharides” — with the pneumococcal serotype(s) not named. The genuine parallel is conceptual: in both kingdoms the capsule is an antiphagocytic virulence factor and the basis of serotyping. That is a parallel, not a relationship.

Other encapsulated bacteria run parallel capsule systems of their own — Neisseria meningitidis serogroups A, B, C, W135, X, Y; Haemophilus influenzae serotypes a–f; Escherichia coli K1; Group B Streptococcus serotypes. Parallels, not relatives: meningococcal “B” ≠ pneumococcal “6B” ≠ cryptococcal “B.”

Discriminative audit — disputed items, checked one by one

Each item below started as a claim that two sources stated differently, or that rested on a single source. For each one, a deeper search was run before anything was settled — and where the deeper search still didn't settle it, the item stays flagged instead of being smoothed over.

6.1 · PCV20: “15B/C” or “15B”? Settled

A reporting convention, not a composition difference. PCV20's antigen is 15B; “15B/C” in surveillance means the assay cannot reliably split 15B and 15C, which interconvert. Write “15B (reported as 15B/C in surveillance).”

6.2 · PPSV23 “20” vs PCV21 “20A” — same or different? Documented

The same antigen under old vs new nomenclature. PPSV23's “20” is 20A in modern terms — the only 20A strain described globally is the PPSV23 strain, and 20A is rare while 20B is common. 20B and the newer 20C are in neither licensed vaccine.

6.3 · PCV21: 21 or 22 serotypes? “8 unique” or “11 added”? Documented, wording trap

Formulation = 21 polysaccharides including de-O-acetylated 15B. Disease coverage = 22 for invasive disease (15B + 15C), 21 for pneumonia (15C, not 15B). “8 unique” counts against any other licensed vaccine; “11 added” counts against PCV20 only. Both numbers are correct under different denominators — state the denominator every time. Separately: a Canadian summary once listed 6C in PCV21's invasive-disease line, but the current FDA insert does not — 6C is excluded here. 6C: partially documented, jurisdiction-specific

6.4 · Is “PCV10” one vaccine? Is Synflorix's 18C on tetanus or diphtheria? Documented

The singular “PCV10” is refuted: two products, four serotypes different. The Synflorix carrier mapping (18C → tetanus toxoid, 19F → diphtheria toxoid, the other eight → protein D) is consistent across the EMA decision, the Egyptian assessment report, and the SmPC — an earlier secondary summary that blurred it was not relied on.

6.5 · Serogroup 6: is 6E a serotype? Are 6F/6G/6H real? 6E refuted

“6E” is not a separate serotype — its cps locus produces polysaccharide identical to 6B (it is 6B class 2 / 6Bii). Any table with 6E as its own row is wrong. 6F and 6G are documented hybrid serotypes; 6H exists in listings but the SeroBA v2.0 tool cannot identify it, so routine identification is not established. 6H: partially documented

6.6 · Serogroup 15: four members or five? Documented: five

Five since 2021, when 15D was added with its own factor reactivity. Any four-member list (15A, 15B, 15C, 15F) is pre-2021 and must not be copied without the update. 15B and 15C interconvert by reversible switching of one gene (wciZ, O-acetylation on/off) — which is exactly why PCV21's de-O-acetylated 15B reaches both.

6.7 · Per-serotype Latin disease names Gap

Targeted searches of Latin disease phrases, every vaccine label, organism-level disease lists, and the Austrian-syndrome literature found no per-serotype Latin nomenclature. The gap is stated, not filled — see the Latin names section.

6.8 · Bacterial/fungal relationships by serogroup Narrowly documented, broadly gap

Documented only in the narrow Mitis-group form in the Relationships section. No source maps pneumococcal serogroups onto fungal serotypes, and no source documents Mitis homologues for most vaccine serotypes.

6.9 · The candidate single-source table — is it complete? Partially documented, demonstrably incomplete

Read in full, not just as a search snippet: two pages, serogroups 1–47, Table 2 “Info coming soon!”, no references. Checked group-by-group against the SSI latex-pool list (which includes serogroup 48 and omits 11E) and the 2025 list of nine new serotypes. Usable as a candidate for the single-source table — never as a verified complete table, and never asserted to be the actual source, since the serotype in question was never named.

6.10 · Are groups 1, 3, 14, 31 really single? Documented, with caveats

No second member appears for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 14 or 31 in any retrieved source. Caveats kept: serotype 14's cps locus is genetically related to serogroup 15 despite no serological cross-reaction, and serotype 3 is a structural outlier made by a synthase pathway unlike most serotypes. “Single” means single in the Danish/Quellung system as retrieved.

6.11 · PCV20 children's otitis media; PCV15 pneumonia wording Partially documented

Only secondary summaries were retrieved for the PCV20 paediatric otitis-media claim, and PCV15's pneumonia/otitis wording splits by jurisdiction. Both are flagged in the Latin names table, not asserted as settled — re-check the current FDA labels before using either in print.

6.12 · “Serotype 20” in old papers vs “20A/20B/20C” in new ones Documented nomenclature trap

Pipeline work reclassified a former “20B variant” as 20C in 2024 — so a 2023 table saying “20B” and a 2026 table saying “20C” may describe the same antigen, not two. Always date-stamp serogroup-20 entries and state which naming era the source uses.

Open questions — stated plainly

  • Which serotype in the earlier table came from the single website? Never named, never guessed here. The check to perform: compare that table cell by cell against the candidate table and against SSI/SeroBA. Any cell matching the candidate alone — especially 11E, a missing serogroup 48, or a pre-2021 four-member group 15, two-member group 20, or five-member group 33 — is the likeliest candidate. That is a check, not a finding.
  • A complete 107-serotype-by-group table was not verified in this research. Re-sourcing targets: the SeroBAnk atlas and SSI's Key to pneumococcal factor sera (5th ed.).
  • Per-serotype Latin disease names and per-serotype fungal relationships were not found — and may not exist as documented concepts. Do not assume a missing source rather than a non-existent mapping.
  • 7D, 24B, 24C, 6H, 10D, 33E/G/H, 36A/B and 33X are documented as existing, but their structures and serology beyond that are a gap in the retrieved text.
  • PCV20 paediatric otitis media and PCV15's global pneumonia/otitis wording remain partially documented (§6.11).
  • PCV21 and 6C — the Canadian line vs the current FDA insert; jurisdiction-specific, excluded from the main list pending a current Canadian monograph check.
  • Mitis homologues for 2, 4, 9A, 13, 23A rest on a second paper not opened in full. Re-open it before quoting.
