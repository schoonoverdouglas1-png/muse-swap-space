## Comparison with other serogroup systems

I did not search for these parallels, so this section is from background knowledge and should be checked against primary sources:

- **Meningococcus (A, B, C, W, Y, X):** Serogroups are defined by capsule chemistry. Group B's capsule is a polysialic acid, which is poorly immunogenic and chemically resembles human neural tissue, so vaccines use other antigens. This is a clear example of molecular similarity not translating into vaccine commonality.
- **Hib (a–f):** Capsule types defined by sugar chemistry. Only type b is a major disease cause, and vaccines target type b alone.
- **Salmonella and E. coli (O:H):** O antigens come from lipopolysaccharide and H antigens from flagella. Serovars are defined by antigenic formula, which often diverges from genomic relatedness.
- **Cryptococcus (A, B, C, D, AD):** Types reflect capsule polysaccharide differences and track with species varieties, so the grouping partly reflects genetics.
- **Group B streptococcus (Ia–IX):** Nine capsular types, with vaccine candidates typically targeting several of them. Type coverage is a key design question.
- **Measles:** One serotype, so antigenic variation is not the main design issue. Genotypes exist but are molecular, not serological, and they do not define vaccine coverage.
